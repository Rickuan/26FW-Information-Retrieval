December 26, 2008

Merry (Pittsburgh) Holidays


In addition to doing the customary Hepner family Christmas celebrations, yesterday brought about the yearly addition of my friend Justin's annual Merry (Pittsburgh) Christmas e-card. So for the past 4 years, Justin and I have been talking about how great it would be to have a Baby Jesus in a Steeler jersey. We've been searching the local yards hoping to come across one, as Pittsburghers are VERY obsessed with the Steelers... Having not found one, thanks to the help of ebay, a baby-sized Steeler shirt, terrible towl, and Heinz Field, Justin's Christmas photo wish came true!



The blow up manger scenes seem to be a new recurrence in my neighborhood. This one was in front of one of the numerous churches that line my street!


But besides this great photo opp, I was astonished to see giant, plastic Menorahs on the roofs of SUV's in Squirrel Hill on Monday. So we stalked the neighborhood (for about 30 seconds) and found one! Then we drove down the same street and found a treasure trove of them! There were at least 6 of them on parked SUV's and minivans! The best part of these were that they had electrical systems that made the bulbs light up when the cars were turned on!

December 8, 2008

CM 2008

So the results of the Top 50 for Photolucida's Critical Mass 2008 are in... and I'm not one of them. I had this hunch before I applied after receiving comments from fellow participants at Review Santa Fe that it was unusual that my work (conceptual installation work) was accepted. I was, and still am, proud to have been a finalist for Critical Mass, but knew that it would be difficult for the type of work that I do to place within the Top 50.

It was only after reading on one of the many photo blogs last week that I discovered the scoring system for CM. "Wow" = 3 points, "I'm glad that I saw this" = 1 point, "No" = 0 points. I had figured that my particular brand of fine art photography (conceptual, scientifically related, abstract, large-scale, digital manipulated) would not be what many of the judges would be looking for when judging the contest. I'm hoping that a few of the 200+ decided it was a "Wow!". It seems doubtful that a majority of them would say that, being that most of the field of photographers that have placed in the Top 50 in recent years have been much about locations, groups of people, etc. and many of the judges are looking for photographers to do editorial assignments and the like. I don't think an installation artist has been up there in the Top 50's, at least according to the Photolucida website. We'll see what the exposure to 200+ photography professionals does to my show record for 2009-2010.

When I get some time, that is, after the semester is over and I've turned in my grades, and made progress on my new project, I'll be posting what I found to be highlights from the CD that the entrants received for CM 2008.

Until then, here's one of my photos that didn't make the cut: Larem:Semaphore

December 3, 2008

2008 Silver Eye Fellowship

I spent part of the unusually sunny day driving over to the South Side to drop off my piece for the Silver Eye Fellowship exhibition that opens next Saturday. It was slightly surreal to be walking down Carson St. with my piece, as it was one of my high school haunts. I'm not sure how I feel about it, other than noticing that more of the old junkshops near Silver Eye now have pending Liquor Licenses on their windows. I thought it would take a few more years for the gentrification from South Side Works to make it down to the 10th St. Bridge. The landscape sure has changed since I wandered around with my handy K100!

I am excited to have been chosen as one of the Honorable Mentions for the show. Other to having a nice little check to cash, I'm looking forward to seeing the work of the others in the show. The winner of the 2008 Fellowship is Martin Weber whose series, A Map of Latin American Dreams, will be featured in the exhibition.

A few of the names I'm familiar with, a few others I've done some google stalking on and found work that I'm proud to be in an exhibition with.

Honorable Mention Photographers:

Sadly, all of the work was leaning against the walls, still wrapped in bubble wrap, so I didn't get any previews. The good news is that there is now an official time for the opening reception, which is Saturday, December 12th from 3-5 at the Silver Eye Center for Photography (1015 East Carson Street). I was informed that the post cards were printed today, so hopefully all of you out there that are on the mailing list will have them in your hands by early next week. Before then, pencil in the date... I'll be there.

December 1, 2008

Darwin Letters

I'm working on a new project that incorporates binary code, evolution, and intelligent design through text that will be "evolving" and "de-evolving." The project itself is exciting to me, except for one thing: lace. I'm making the alphabet in the typography of Darwin's Evolution of the Species in silk lace. Each one of these fine letters takes between 3-5 hours to create; I'm on I. The goal for today is to get through I and hopefully start J. Since 2009 is the 150th birthday for Darwin, having the photography started will be one step closer to finishing an installation that does not yet have a home. Cheers, time to drink some coffee!
